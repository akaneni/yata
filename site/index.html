A notch for Windows

A quiet messenger at the top of your screen.

Yata sits just below your camera. It shows what's playing, your next meeting and your reminders, then gets out of the way.

Download for Windows Read the source
Open source, MIT No account No telemetry
What it carries

Small things, one glance away.

Closed, it's a black pill with the time. Hover it and it opens into tabs. It hides when you go full screen.

Media

Now playing, from any app

Spotify, YouTube in the browser, anything that uses Windows media controls. Cover, progress and controls, plus synced lyrics that scroll with the song.

Agenda

Your next meeting

Connect Google Calendar, read-only. "Standup in 8 min" appears in the closed notch, and Join opens Meet, Zoom or Teams.

Focus

A Pomodoro you can see

25 minutes of focus, 5 of rest, a long break every four. The countdown stays in the closed notch, and it pauses itself when you walk away.

Water and tasks

Small habits, kept

A daily water goal with gentle nudges. A task list where "Call Ana 15:30" becomes a reminder, with optional two-way sync to Google Tasks.

Shelf

A shelf for files

Drop files on the notch, drag them back out into any app later. Only the path is kept, nothing is copied.

System

What Windows is doing

Volume, brightness, battery, audio output, Caps Lock, keyboard layout and network. A dot lights up when an app is using your microphone.

八咫烏

Named after a messenger.

In Japanese myth, Yatagarasu is the three-legged crow sent to guide the way. It's a cousin of Huginn and Muninn, Odin's ravens, who fly over the world and come back with news.

Yata does the same from the top of your screen. A 16 × 14 pixel raven, drawn by hand, lives in the notch and reacts to your day.

It never sees what you type

Caps Lock and keyboard layout are read by checking key state. There's no keyboard hook.

No account, no telemetry

The diagnostic log stays on your PC for 7 days, and it leaves out songs, tasks, events, file paths and accounts.
